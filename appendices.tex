\chapter{Two-Stream Reference Specification}\label{app:spec}

This appendix specifies a minimal two-stream system, sufficient for a first prototype of the kind described in Appendix~E.

\emph{Display.} A projector or emissive screen refreshing at $R=144$ slots per second, with a controllable blanking interval of duration $\beta$ at the start of each slot. Streams occupy period $p=2$: stream 0 in even slots, stream 1 in odd slots. Each stream receives 72 flashes per second; content captured at 24 images per second is shown three times per image, or content captured at 72 images per second once each.

\emph{Gating.} Each lens follows the gate $g_{2,\varphi}$ of \Cref{ch:phase}, opening after the blanking interval of each admitted slot and closing before the next slot begins. With slot duration $1/R\approx 6.94$ ms, the open interval is at most $1/R-\beta$, less the shutter's switching time.

\emph{Adaptive gating.} When the coincidence map marks a slot as coincident, meaning the displayed images of both streams agree within the declared perceptual tolerance, the glasses remain open through it. Transitions between gated and open operation are scheduled at cuts or ramped over at least several hundred milliseconds, and are never repeated at a regular interval.

\emph{Synchronization.} A beacon transmits, at least once per frame period, a packet carrying the slot counter, the current coincidence flag for the next several slots, the current protection level, and a schedule identifier. Glasses maintain a local oscillator locked to the slot counter, so that brief loss of the beacon does not lose phase.

\emph{Selection.} A two-position dial sets $\varphi\in\{0,1\}$. The glasses store only the dial position and, if the exhibition permits switching under the switch system, the current switch state of \Cref{ch:switch-admissibility}.

\emph{Failure.} On loss of the beacon beyond the oscillator's holdover time, the glasses enter the declared failure mode (transparent, opaque, or common-stream), which the beacon's protection level may specify per passage.

\emph{Excluded components.} The glasses contain no camera, eye tracker, microphone, general-purpose storage, or transmitter. This exclusion is part of the specification, not an option.

\chapter{Variant-Manuscript Notation}\label{app:notation}

A variant manuscript records the spine and columns of \Cref{ch:matrix}. Each production unit is written as a block with a shared header and one column per realization. A minimal plain-text form follows.

\begin{verbatim}
UNIT 14  "The control room"
  ENVELOPE      00:41:10 - 00:43:55
  ANCHORS       00:41:10 door opens (shared sound)
                00:43:55 lights fail (shared sound)
  SPINE         Theseus enters; overrides the broadcast console;
                the dome lights fail.
  COLUMN RNP    Played for dread. Close on Theseus's hands.
                ESTABLISHES  console_overridden
                PRESUPPOSES  knows_override_code
  COLUMN BNP    Played as a prank. Wide, with the guard asleep.
                ESTABLISHES  console_overridden
                PRESUPPOSES  knows_override_code
  DIALOGUE      Faces speaking in both columns: "It's done."
                (single recorded line; shared track)
  COINCIDENT    final 4 s (lights fail, both columns)
  RECONCILE     none; rendezvous at end of unit
  PROTECTION    casual
\end{verbatim}

The fields correspond to the tuple $\langle A_i,V_i,\Delta_i,Q_i\rangle$: ANCHORS and ENVELOPE to $A_i$, the COLUMN entries to $V_i$, ESTABLISHES and PRESUPPOSES to $\Delta_i$, and RECONCILE to $Q_i$. DIALOGUE, COINCIDENT, and PROTECTION feed the dialogue check, the coincidence map, and the protection profile.

\chapter{Exhibition Manifest}\label{app:manifest}

The manifest of \Cref{ch:persuasion} is published with the variant registry for every screening. A minimal form follows; values in parentheses are placeholders.

\begin{verbatim}
WORK            Rarely/Barely Needed Protocols
REGISTRY        (public registry identifier)
SCREENING       (venue, date, start time)
DISPLAY         144 slots/s, period 2, adaptive gating on
SOUND           complementary score, shared
STREAM 0        realization RNP  (thriller)   phase 0
                authors: (credits)   fingerprint: (hash)
STREAM 1        realization BNP  (satire)     phase 1
                authors: (credits)   fingerprint: (hash)
COMPOSITE       designed; fingerprint: (hash)
SWITCHING       structural rendezvous only
FAILURE MODE    transparent (casual passages),
                opaque (protected passages)
SPONSORED       none
DISTRIBUTIVE    none
\end{verbatim}

A viewer reads the phase on their own glasses and finds the corresponding stream in the manifest. Nothing about the viewer is recorded.

\chapter{Evaluation Protocol}\label{app:protocol}

The protocol follows the four groups of \Cref{ch:evaluation}. Technical tests measure refresh stability, phase drift over a full screening, optical crosstalk, informational legibility of leaked frames at protected moments, luminance through the glasses in coincident and divergent passages, central and peripheral flicker, the timing and regularity of brightness changes, performance across seating positions, and behavior under loss of beacon and power. Narrative tests watch each realization alone, review the family's relations and asymmetries, attempt admissible and forbidden switches including multi-switch paths, and compare the memories of viewers of different realizations. Social tests ask mixed audiences whether they attended one event, how they experienced others' reactions, how the conversation afterward went, and whether they felt ranked. The ethical audit inspects the glasses for excluded components, checks the manifest against the exhibited streams, confirms provenance, and reviews the disclosure given to the audience. A family is not exhibited until all four groups pass.

\chapter{Prototype Film}\label{app:prototype}

A short experimental production would cross three dimensions, each with two options: genre, comedy or drama; explanation, beginner or expert; ending, A or B. The nominal space contains eight realizations. The prototype would not show all eight in one screening, which Part~I rules out, but would produce all eight and exhibit them in pairs and triples.

Its purpose is to measure the couplings \Cref{ch:divergence} predicted. Does changing genre change the time available for explanation? Does each ending remain earned in both genres, or does one ending need different seeds in the comic and dramatic realizations? How far do the eight realizations actually differ in image, fact, and time distance, and how far does the nominal count of eight overstate the family? The answers would show whether dimensions that are analytically independent can be made operationally independent, and at what cost.

\chapter{Glossary}\label{app:glossary}

\begin{description}
\item[Adaptive gating] Opening the glasses fully while streams coincide on screen, so that multiplexing costs are paid only during divergence.
\item[Blink-rate glasses] Glasses whose lenses open and close on a schedule synchronized to the display; the rate belongs to the shutter, not the wearer.
\item[Coincidence] Agreement of streams' displayed images within a declared perceptual tolerance.
\item[Common channel] Any element of a screening received identically by everyone present.
\item[Composite] What the unaided eye sees: the time-weighted average, in linear light, of all streams.
\item[Envelope] An interval on the common clock within which every realization must begin and end a scene.
\item[Occasion-preserving family] A family exhibited with bounded drift, recurring structural rendezvous, and a common channel.
\item[Period and phase] The two integers that fix a periodic gate: how many slots per cycle, and which slot opens.
\item[Protection profile] The schedule of leakage requirements across a film.
\item[Realization] One complete audiovisual experience within a family.
\item[Rendezvous, structural] A time at which every realization establishes the same relevant facts.
\item[Rendezvous, viewer-relative] A time at which a particular history admits a switch into every realization.
\item[Selection and concealment] Choosing a stream, and preventing someone from seeing one; different functions with different obligations.
\item[Switch admissibility] The condition that a history covers what a continuation presupposes and does not contradict what it commits to.
\item[Switch system] The transition system whose states summarize viewers' histories for purposes of switching.
\item[Temporal debt] A realization's displacement from the spine on the common clock.
\item[Variant family] A set of realizations designed for synchronized presentation.
\item[Variant matrix] The writing format of spine and columns that records a family's relations.
\end{description}
